The conclusion the answer states agrees with the numbers the answer itself
gives.

If it says 18 catch blocks were examined and 2 swallow the error, then it must
not also claim to be unsure whether others exist, and must not present a count
of judged sites that differs from the count of sites found without explaining
the difference.

A conclusion that disagrees with its own arithmetic fails even when the
conclusion happens to be correct. Agreeing by luck is the failure this is
measuring: an answer whose numbers and whose claim do not line up gives a reader
no way to tell which of the two to believe.

Passes if every number in the answer is consistent with every other and with the
final claim.

Fails if the totals do not add up, if the answer hedges the completeness of a
set it has just counted, or if it reports a count and then draws a conclusion
that a different count would be needed to support.
